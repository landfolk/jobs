# Engineering Levels at Landfolk

This document describes the leveling framework for Software Engineers at Landfolk. It's adapted from [Basecamp's programmer titles](https://basecamp.com/handbook/titles-for-programmers), adjusted for our context, team size, and ways of working.

## Overview

We have 7 levels, spanning from student positions through to principal-level technical leadership:

| Level | Title |
| :---- | :---- |
| L1 | Assistant Software Engineer |
| L2 | Junior Software Engineer |
| L3 | Software Engineer |
| L4 | Software Engineer |
| L5 | Senior Software Engineer |
| L6 | Lead Software Engineer |
| L7 | Principal Software Engineer |

Each level is evaluated across three dimensions:

1. **Skill** — Technical ability, scope of work, judgment, tooling, leadership, and business context  
2. **Engagement** — Ownership, communication, planning, and trust  
3. **Coachability** — How you give and receive feedback

---

## Assistant Software Engineer (L1)

Entry-level position for students or very early-career engineers. The focus is on learning, absorbing patterns, and contributing under close guidance.

### Skill

#### Mastery

Learning fundamental language and framework features. Working through unfamiliar concepts with support. Contributes through a mix of pair programming and independent work, with growing emphasis on self-direction.

#### Scope

Contributes to well-defined, small tasks with clear specifications and guidance.

#### Judgment

Asks good questions. Seeks to understand why things are done a certain way before jumping in. Attempts to solve problems but knows when to ask for help early.

#### Tooling

Becoming familiar with our development environment, version control, and basic debugging tools.

#### Leadership

Building relationships with teammates. Learning to communicate clearly about progress and blockers.

#### Business Context

Learning what Landfolk does and who our customers are. Understands their immediate team's purpose.

### Engagement

#### Ownership

Completes assigned tasks under supervision. Participates in team rituals and learns from observing how others work.

#### Communication

Proactively shares what they're working on. Asks for help when stuck. Documents learnings for their own reference.

#### Planning

Works on tasks assigned by others. Learning to estimate how long things take.

#### Trust

All work is thoroughly reviewed. Significant guidance and pairing expected. Success is measured by growth rate and quality of questions asked.

### Coachability

#### Feedback

Actively seeks feedback and receives it with a growth mindset. Observes and learns from the work and behavior of others.

## Junior Software Engineer (L2)

Capable of working independently on routine problems. Still learning the codebase and building confidence.

### Skill

#### Mastery

Proficient in basic language and framework features, but some advanced structures may still be unfamiliar. Occasional issues following patterns within existing code bases.

#### Scope

Capable of working on tightly-scoped, routine problems.

#### Judgment

Demonstrates curiosity in their craft. Prioritizes their own development. Researches what's been done before and why. Attempts to solve problems independently.

#### Tooling

Has a working understanding of our development tools, monitoring, and project management systems.

#### Leadership

Can articulate thoughts clearly, listen actively, and problem solve with others. Building strong relationships within the team.

#### Business Context

Understands how their team's work contributes to the product. Beginning to learn how other teams (operations, community, marketing) interact with what engineering builds.

### Engagement

#### Ownership

Participates in goalie rotation (our rotating on-call for production issues) under guidance from more senior engineers, handling simpler issues end-to-end.

#### Communication

Proactively communicates what they're working on. Attempts to unblock themselves but asks for help when needed.

#### Planning

Prioritizes daily work against team/project needs with regular guidance. Demonstrates good time management.

#### Trust

Work is thoroughly reviewed with back-and-forth frequently needed before merging. Reviews pull requests within familiar patterns. Learning to give constructive feedback. Makes minor contributions to documentation.

### Coachability

#### Feedback

Observes and learns from the work and behavior of others. Receives constructive feedback with a growth mindset.

## Software Engineer I (L3)

A capable, productive engineer who works well within existing systems and patterns.

### Skill

#### Mastery

Follows established patterns and approaches within existing code bases with ease. Produces code that is considered high quality by colleagues. Effectively applies domain design skills in normal course of work.

#### Scope

Capable of working on clearly defined and scoped individual features or problems.

#### Judgment

Proactively identifies and resolves problems. Improves areas of the code they work on. Leaves things in better shape than they found them. Pays attention to detail.

#### Tooling

Uses our common tools in day-to-day work to diagnose issues and optimize performance.

#### Leadership

Identifies and addresses the needs of the team through creating tools, documentation, and process that improve team effectiveness. Models humility and ownership in service of the team's long-term success.

#### Business Context

Understands how their features connect to user needs and business goals. Knows who the stakeholders are for their work and can incorporate their feedback.

### Engagement

#### Ownership

Participates in goalie shifts with guidance from a more senior team member. Capable of handling or triaging production issues across multiple systems with minimal assistance. Can lead the resolution of bugs or minor incidents related to their work.

#### Communication

Presents pull requests that demonstrate competency with our patterns and principles, but may require oversight from senior peers.

#### Planning

Prioritizes tasks and project work with little direction. Works confidently and effectively. Persists when faced with uncertainty but pulls in support when needed. Contributes to cycle planning within the team.

#### Trust

Work is reviewed with some back-and-forth. Occasional need for direction or implementation changes. Regularly reviews pull requests. Gives clear, constructive feedback. Makes material contributions to documentation.

### Coachability

#### Feedback

Provides specific, well-considered feedback within their area of expertise. Able to disagree and commit.

---

## Software Engineer II (L4)

An experienced engineer who can take substantial features from concept to shipping. Sought out for advice by peers.

### Skill

#### Mastery

Capable of devising new patterns and conventions within existing code bases and in greenfield development. Code remains accurate, reliable, and maintainable even when faced with challenging problems.

#### Scope

Capable of taking substantial features from concept to shipping as the primary engineer (alongside a designer).

#### Judgment

Has accumulated sufficient experience to be consistently sought by peers, across teams, for advice. Improves existing professional standards for the team.

#### Tooling

Demonstrates a comprehensive understanding of our tools. Uses them regularly to diagnose issues quickly and calmly, even under pressure.

#### Leadership

Provides guidance to teammates. Can act as an unofficial mentor to newer engineers. Works well with anyone. Navigates difficult situations directly and with ease.

#### Business Context

Understands cross-team dependencies and how engineering decisions affect other parts of the organization. Can translate technical concepts for non-technical stakeholders. Builds relationships outside engineering.

### Engagement

#### Ownership

Can recognize and raise major incidents and lead the response from inception to resolution.

#### Communication

Trusted to provide actionable feedback across team pull requests that requires little or no oversight. Provides technical advice and suggestions during planning and scoping.

#### Planning

Requires no direction to effectively manage their time. Finds valuable and productive tasks to work on without prompting. Helps steer cycle planning. Fosters collaboration across teams.

#### Trust

Work doesn't necessarily need to be reviewed, but general approach may be discussed. A frequent reviewer. Gives clear, constructive feedback on quality and maintainability. Produces and maintains documentation, runbooks, and pitches.

### Coachability

#### Feedback

Provides specific, constructive feedback on the work of L1-L3 engineers that deepens their knowledge across broad areas.

---

## Senior Software Engineer (L5)

A technical leader who owns significant systems and mentors others. Defines standards and can lead teams through multi-week projects.

### Skill

#### Mastery

Acts as a subject matter expert on multiple systems. Makes strategic decisions around major components.

#### Scope

Capable of owning and running entire subsystems of work. Strong track record of introducing significant improvements in system quality, performance, or scalability.

#### Judgment

Has the experience, trust, and respect to operate at the tech lead level. Defines professional standards. Is sought for advice across disciplines.

#### Tooling

An expert in our tools. Extends and improves them over time. Can identify where tooling should be improved and guide implementation.

#### Leadership

A mentor and coach who actively develops others through regular, constructive feedback. Capable of mentoring 1-3 engineers. Helps define performance standards. People outside your team seek your guidance. A leader on the team, technically and culturally.

#### Business Context

Strong understanding of Landfolk's business model and how different teams operate. Prioritizes work based on business impact. Works directly with stakeholders across the organization to shape solutions.

### Engagement

#### Ownership

Can lead the resolution of any engineering problem in their domain. Is a significant technical contributor to problem resolution. Demonstrates consistent maturity in communication under pressure.

#### Communication

Provides critical, actionable feedback on pull requests which ensures quality and consistency across the team. Demonstrates project management best practices. Ensures leadership is informed on team progress.

#### Planning

Can plan entire cycles for a team. Can drive the agenda, hammer scope, and offer critical insight from extensive experience. Regularly challenges the team to help drive projects forward.

#### Trust

Major projects can be completed and shipped without review. Provides guidance to senior, mid, and junior engineers. Expected to review complex and high-impact pull requests. Defines and maintains standards by developing processes and procedures.

### Coachability

#### Feedback

Consistently sought by colleagues for assistance with critical work or decisions.

---

## Lead Software Engineer (L6)

A senior technical leader who owns cross-cutting concerns and shapes technical direction across the organization. Leads multi-cycle initiatives.

### Skill

#### Mastery

Deep expertise across multiple domains. Creates new systems and architectural patterns that become organizational standards. Solves problems that span team boundaries.

#### Scope

Owns technical direction for large systems or cross-cutting concerns (performance, security, developer experience, etc.). Leads initiatives that span multiple cycles.

#### Judgment

Trusted to make significant technical decisions with organization-wide impact. Balances technical ideals with pragmatic business needs. Known for sound judgment in ambiguous situations.

#### Tooling

Shapes the tooling strategy. Evaluates build-vs-buy decisions. Can objectively rationalize tradeoffs with business strategy in mind. Beginning to evaluate emerging technologies and their potential fit for the organization.

#### Leadership

Mentors senior engineers. Actively develops others through teaching and example. Elevates the technical capabilities of the entire organization. Capable of leading larger technical initiatives with multiple contributors. Sets the bar for engineering excellence.

#### Business Context

Deep understanding of how all parts of the organization work together. Influences product direction with technical insight. Represents engineering in cross-functional discussions. Trusted by stakeholders outside engineering.

### Engagement

#### Ownership

Leads the most complex technical initiatives. Takes ownership of organization-wide technical health. Proactively identifies and addresses technical debt and systemic issues.

#### Communication

Communicates technical vision and strategy to both technical and non-technical stakeholders. Creates clarity in complex situations. Documents architectural decisions and their rationale. May begin contributing externally through writing, talks, or open source.

#### Planning

Plans and coordinates work across multiple teams and cycles. Identifies dependencies and risks early. Helps leadership understand technical implications of business decisions. Beginning to think about longer-term technical strategy.

#### Trust

Entire systems can be designed and shipped without review. Provides architectural oversight across the organization. Expected to review system architecture and ensure work meets the highest technical standards.

### Coachability

#### Feedback

Defines engineering standards and guidance. Sought by colleagues across the organization for assistance with critical work or decisions.

---

## Principal Software Engineer (L7)

The highest individual contributor level. Shapes technical strategy for the entire organization. May represent Landfolk's engineering externally.

### Skill

#### Mastery

Creates new systems and approaches that significantly advance the organization's capabilities. Deep expertise that spans the entire technical stack. Recognized as an authority in their domain.

#### Scope

Sets the strategic technical direction for the organization. Works on the highest-complexity problems. The go-to person for advice on the most technically challenging decisions.

#### Judgment

A leader across the organization, technically and culturally. Capable of setting and directing technical strategy. Known company-wide as the expert for advice on critical technical decisions.

#### Tooling

Shapes the long-term technical platform strategy. Evaluates emerging technologies and their potential impact on the organization.

#### Leadership

A leader at the company, technically and culturally. Capable of leading major technical initiatives with organization-wide impact. Elevates the entire engineering organization through teaching, writing, and example.

#### Business Context

Shapes how technology enables business strategy. Trusted advisor to leadership on how technical decisions affect company direction. Understands market dynamics and competitive landscape as they relate to technical choices.

### Engagement

#### Ownership

Fully capable of leading the most critical technical challenges to successful resolution. Takes ownership of the organization's technical health and evolution.

#### Communication

Demonstrates long-term strategic thinking. Works with leadership to communicate engineering vision across the organization. May represent Landfolk externally through writing, speaking, or open source contributions.

#### Planning

Capable of planning and coordinating large, long-running initiatives. Elevates organizational standards through developing new systems, processes, and effective communication. Pushes the organization forward through implementing new systems and designs.

#### Trust

Entire systems can be designed, developed, and shipped autonomously. Provides architectural oversight across the organization. Ensures robust and effective best practices are understood and followed across engineering.

### Coachability

#### Feedback

Defines organization-wide engineering standards and guidance. Consistently sought by colleagues across the organization for assistance with critical work or decisions. Actively develops the next generation of technical leaders.

## Notes on Application

### This is a framework, not a checklist

Not every bullet point needs to be met perfectly at each level. Use this as a guide for conversations about growth and expectations, not as a rigid scorecard.

### Levels describe sustained performance

Promotion reflects that someone is already operating at the next level consistently, not that they've hit a few high points.

### Growth isn't always vertical

Moving from L4 to L5 isn't the only way to grow. Deepening expertise, expanding scope within a level, or taking on different types of challenges are all valid forms of growth.

### Context matters

A Senior at Landfolk operates in a small, high-trust environment with significant autonomy. That context shapes what "senior" means here versus at a 1000-person company.

### The L4 → L5 transition (becoming Senior)

The shift from Software Engineer to Senior is often the biggest jump. It's less about technical skill (which should already be strong) and more about:

- Taking responsibility for outcomes beyond your own code  
- Actively developing others, not just helping when asked  
- Defining standards rather than following them  
- Leading through ambiguity when the path isn't clear

### Levels and Shape Up

Levels don't determine who works on what. An L3 and L5 (Senior) might be on the same cycle team, with different expectations:

- The Senior might drive technical direction and unblock the L3  
- The L3 contributes meaningfully and grows from the experience  
- Both are accountable for shipping

Team composition considers skills needed, not just levels.
